Site context · Fullerton Town Center

Locate the redevelopment within the shopping center

Atlas Fullerton (also marketed as Atlas Condesa) is located at 229 East Orangethorpe Avenue, Fullerton, CA, northwest of South Lemon Street and Orangethorpe Avenue in Fullerton Town Center. Costco and AMC are nearby shopping-center landmarks [1, 4, 6].

Start with the investment decision

What had to happen before construction could begin?

The project replaces commercial buildings and parking with a five-story apartment community and ground-floor retail. The residential building wraps a parking structure. Existing buildings are being replaced; this is a change in land use rather than the reuse of a store's interior.

The land covered by the development

The development site is about 4.5 acres

The city’s initial study identifies a 4.47-acre project area, or approximately 4.5 acres. With 329 homes, the planned density is about 73.6 homes per acre. This is the land area used in the case [1].

The study describes the site using portions of multiple assessor parcels. A development boundary and an individual tax-parcel boundary need not coincide. The reported $21 million development-site sale is the acquisition anchor used in the model; it is not a sale price for the entire shopping center [1, 5].

2021 traffic analysis · replacement of active commercial uses

Fewer daily trips can coexist with busier peak hours

Many residents leave during the morning commute, when most of the replaced retail uses are closed. In the evening, the old commercial uses already generated trips, so the increase is smaller. The composition and timing of trips change even as their daily sum declines.

These are site trip estimates, not a prediction that all nearby roads become less congested. A vehicle entry or exit counts as one trip end; a visit arriving and departing counts twice. Congestion depends on intersection capacity, turning movements, queues, signal timing, Costco and other shopping traffic, and growth elsewhere in the area.

The study applied no reductions for internal capture or pass-by trips to the proposed project. Internal capture means trips between uses within the development; pass-by trips are stops by vehicles already traveling on the adjacent road. Walkability or fewer separate shopping drives may be relevant in practice, but those mechanisms do not explain the reported reduction in this calculation.

The 2021 plan also included a signal at Lemon Street / Liberty Avenue, southbound right-turn lanes, and restrictions on movements at the project driveway. These proposed roadway changes affect how traffic is handled. Their completion has not been verified in this case.

Second quarter: stronger leasing

Adjustment formula and weights

Adjusted rent = assumed reference rent × (model apartment SF ÷ reference-plan SF)α × (1 + other adjustment / 100). Average available reference examples equally within each bedroom category. Weight the resulting bedroom-category rents by the model’s assumed 80 / 150 / 99 mix.

These are sensitivity assumptions, not statistically estimated coefficients. South Harbor alone supplies no studio reference; it cannot populate all three model rents. The feasibility calculation separately accounts for restricted units.

Commercial leases

What is 5,995 square feet worth to the landlord?

Retail Insite markets divisible ground-floor retail / restaurant space under Atlas Condesa. “Divisible” does not establish a minimum suite size, approved tenant uses, or signed leases. View the broker’s listing and marketing materials [6].

Both offers are hypothetical. Actual asking rent, lease terms, tenant-improvement contributions, free rent, commissions, and expense recovery have not been disclosed in the supplied materials.

For comparison, each offer leases the entire 5,995 SF. All rent and operating-expense inputs are dollars per square foot per year. Tenant improvements (TI) are a one-time landlord contribution per square foot; the leasing commission is a percentage of base rent collected over the full lease.

Compare two offers

Shared assumptions

NNN (triple net): this simplified offer reimburses all modeled operating expenses. Gross: the landlord pays those expenses from rent. In both offers, expenses continue during free base-rent months; under NNN, reimbursement continues too. Rent and expense increases occur at each lease anniversary. Payments arrive at month-end.

MeasureOffer AOffer B

Equivalent net annual rent converts NPV into a constant monthly payment over the same term, then multiplies by 12 and divides by 5,995 SF. It includes the upfront TI and commission. Tenant default, renewal, terminal property value, and income taxes are excluded.

How the lease NPV is calculated

  1. Calculate each month’s rent. First-year scheduled rent = 5,995 SF × annual rent per SF ÷ 12. Set base rent to zero during the initial free months. Apply the annual increase at months 13, 25, and so on.
  2. Calculate the landlord’s net receipt. Monthly cash flow = base rent collected + expense reimbursement − operating expenses.
  3. Discount each month to lease signing. The monthly discount rate is (1 + annual discount rate)1/12 − 1. Divide month m’s net receipt by (1 + monthly rate)m, then sum all discounted receipts.
  4. Deduct the upfront costs. TI = 5,995 × TI per SF. Commission = total undiscounted base rent collected × commission rate. Both are assumed paid at month 0.

Development budget & funding

Cost detail and funding treatment

Eligible cost is the land, hard cost, soft cost, contingency, retail TI, and commission. The construction loan funds a fixed share of those items. Equity funds the remainder and all interest, loan fees, reserves, and delay overhead.

Asking rents are reduced by vacancy and the separate concession allowance. The base teaching scenario assumes zero stabilized concessions; this can be changed in the inputs. Residential expenses include assumed property tax, insurance, payroll, repairs, and management through an expense ratio. Retail expenses are separate. No replacement reserve is deducted from NOI; adjust underwriting accordingly.

How the construction loan works

A construction loan is drawn as eligible project costs are paid. Principal is the amount borrowed and still owed. The commitment is the model’s total loan funding; the initial balance is smaller because most construction spending comes later.

1. Fund the project in stages

Land closes at month 0. Loan and equity fund the selected shares of each eligible cost. Construction spending follows a smooth S-curve: slower early and late, faster in the middle. Retail TI and commission are funded at completion.

2. Pay monthly interest

The assumed annual loan rate divided by 12 is applied to opening principal plus half that month’s new draw. The half-draw convention approximates spending throughout the month. Equity pays interest in cash; interest is not added to principal. No principal is repaid during construction.

3. Repay at the end

At completion, the full principal remains due. The model tests a permanent loan sized to stabilized operations below. Completion and stabilization may occur at different dates; interim lease-up financing, lender covenants, and extension rights are not modeled.

Can a permanent loan repay the construction principal?

Stabilized NOI is annual net operating income after vacancy and operating expenses, before interest and principal payments. Capitalized value = NOI ÷ capitalization rate.

LTV (loan-to-value) limits the permanent loan to a percentage of that modeled property value.

DSCR (debt-service coverage ratio) = annual NOI ÷ annual principal-and-interest payments. A 1.25× minimum means $1.25 of NOI for each $1 of annual debt service.

The loan is the smaller of the LTV limit and the amount supported by NOI at the minimum DSCR. Monthly amortization determines the payment per dollar borrowed. Any shortfall against construction principal requires additional repayment equity in this scenario.

This is a hypothetical sizing calculation, not an available loan quote. It excludes refinancing fees and cash-out distributions. An indicated shortfall is not evidence that the actual borrower faces a funding problem.

Model definitions and limits

Market-unit rent uses the illustrative unit-mix weights. Residential effective gross income deducts vacancy and concessions on occupied base rent, then adds occupied-unit other income. The single concession rate applies across apartment types as a teaching simplification; actual eligibility may differ. NOI adds occupied retail base rent and recoveries, less all modeled retail expenses. Value = stabilized NOI / cap rate. Yield on cost = NOI / total development cost.

Eligible cost = land + hard cost + soft cost + contingency + retail TI + leasing commission. Hard cost per apartment is an all-project proxy including parking and the retail shell; it is not cost per residential square foot. Interest each month = annual rate / 12 × (opening principal + half of that month's draw). Construction debt pays a fixed share of eligible costs; equity pays all interest. Cost includes an additional residential lease-up allowance and retail free-rent reserve.

This is a feasibility screen, not an investment IRR, a complete lease-up model, a quantity-surveyor estimate, or the sponsor's underwriting. It excludes income during construction, phasing, tax benefits, land carry before model month 0, refinancing fees, sale costs, and income taxes. Positive value less cost alone does not prove an adequate risk-adjusted return.
