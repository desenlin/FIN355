Discussion: Why does Year 2 cash flow fall so sharply?

Rent falls 5% while vacancy rises from 7% to 14%. Net rental revenue falls about 12.2%, even before other changes. Lower operating expenses partly offset the decline, but higher property taxes, tenant improvements, and leasing commissions add pressure. The mortgage payment stays fixed.

Why a higher adjusted NOI does not mean free CapEx

Prefunding moves cash into a restricted account at acquisition. Later withdrawals can preserve annual distributable cash, but the money was committed earlier. When the reserve is exhausted, only the uncovered portion of CapEx reduces adjusted NOI.

Uncovered CapEx = max(0, CapEx − beginning reserve − annual contribution)

This is an undiscounted funding identity. Timing, financing, and opportunity cost still matter. The case loan finances 75% of purchase price plus the initial reserve; changing that reserve in a complete investment model would also change the loan and equity.

Discussion: Does prefunding eliminate capital costs?

Try “No prefunding,” then inspect Year 1. The annual contribution covers part of the bill and operating cash funds the remaining amount. Next try “Partial funding”: the initial $10,000 covers part of that shortfall. Compare the cash available with the original case.

How is adjusted cost basis calculated?

Capitalized TIs and CapEx increase basis. Depreciation of the building, TIs, and CapEx reduces it. Leasing-commission amortization is separate.

Two parts of the same gain

Depreciation lowered the tax basis during ownership. This part of the gain reflects those prior deductions.

Property appreciation

The remaining gain is the net sale price above acquisition cost plus capitalized improvements.

Case assumptions and teaching conventions
  • Acquisition and financing: $6.7 million purchase, $225,000 initial reserve, and a six-year hold. The loan funds 75% of purchase price plus the reserve, at 7% interest with 30-year monthly amortization. Equity also pays 1% loan points.
  • Operating cash flow: NOI is measured after the reserve contribution. Adjusted NOI deducts TIs, leasing commissions, and CapEx not covered by reserve withdrawals.
  • Tax assumptions: 21% on operating taxable income, 25% on depreciation recapture, and 15% on property appreciation. Building depreciation spans 27.5 years; TIs and leasing commissions use one year; CapEx and loan points use seven years, with remaining loan points deducted at sale.
  • Sale and experiments: Sale value uses forward adjusted NOI of $793,076.91, with 2% selling costs. The reserve experiment stands alone. Sections 3 and 4 share the same exit cap rate and retain the original reserve and financing.
